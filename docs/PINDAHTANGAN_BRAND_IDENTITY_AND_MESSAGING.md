# PindahTangan — Brand Identity & Messaging System

**Status:** proposed foundation for a controlled Sukabumi pilot.  
**Use for:** product UI, pilot deck, landing page, owned social, and partner conversations.  
**Evidence basis:** product PRD v1.0, existing UI/source review, Product & UX Audit, and Pilot Business Model & Economics Decision Memo. The audit establishes that the current deployment is a local-state prototype and is **not** approved for public or real-money use. This guide therefore separates a durable brand idea from claims that require operating proof.

## 1. Brand in one page

### The job
PindahTangan helps people move good clothes out of an underused wardrobe through a managed consignment process. The service is for someone who sees value in her clothes but does not want the second job of listing, negotiating, packing, and shipping them one by one.

### The human tension
"Barangnya masih bagus. Tapi kalau harus jual sendiri, ya nanti saja."  
That “nanti” turns into a crowded wardrobe, not because the owner does not care, but because the work of resale is fragmented and draining.

### Brand promise
**PindahTangan helps usable clothes move on with a clearer, lighter process.**

It should earn this promise through visible process clarity: what is accepted, what happens next, what payout is agreed, and when an update is due. It does not earn trust with vague sustainability language, vintage props, or inflated income claims.

### Positioning
For busy Sukabumi wardrobe owners who want to let go of usable clothes without individually reselling them, **PindahTangan is a managed fashion-consignment service** that coordinates intake, curation, merchandising, and sale progress in one process. It feels like a capable local team handling a task people keep postponing.

### Single-minded message
**Good clothes deserve a next owner; you do not have to handle every step yourself.**

### Brand character
| Trait | In practice | Not this |
|---|---|---|
| Capable | Specific steps, named statuses, clear next action | Stiff, bureaucratic, over-explaining |
| Considered | Clean edit, thoughtful details, no visual clutter | Pretending to be a luxury fashion house |
| Warm | Human, local, easy to ask questions | Cutesy, overly maternal, sentimental |
| Straightforward | Clear acceptance, price, and payout language | “Cuan” hype or hidden mechanics |
| Lively | Clothes in motion, real hands and real handling | Dead, clinical sustainability imagery |

### Core audiences
1. **Primary — wardrobe owner / consignor:** Women approximately 24–40 in Sukabumi; working, running a household, or both. She has usable pieces, little spare time, and low appetite for marketplace admin. Her first question is: “Ini jelas proses dan hasilnya nggak?”
2. **Secondary — live-fashion buyer:** Women approximately 18–35 who enjoy discovery and value. She needs clear condition, fit, and price information; do not position her as someone who should tolerate uncertainty because it is “thrift.”
3. **Operational audience — host, pickup, studio, and partner:** They need a brand that makes the process feel organized and respectful to the clothes’ owner. Their language should be functional and unambiguous.

## 2. Name and positioning test

### Recommended name treatment: **PindahTangan**
The name is memorable, Indonesian, active, and naturally describes circulation without sounding like a recycling program. It has a useful double meaning: an item literally changes hands, and its owner can let someone else carry the selling work.

Use the joined form **PindahTangan** in logos, UI, and headings. Use “PindahTangan” as a proper noun in sentence copy. Do not add “Atelier,” “Collective,” “Circular Marketplace,” or “Sustainable Chic” as a public-facing masterbrand name; these make a young local pilot sound less direct.

### Descriptor hierarchy
- **Primary descriptor:** Konsinyasi fesyen terkelola
- **Support descriptor:** Untuk pakaian yang masih layak dipakai, dari lemari ke pemilik berikutnya
- **Pilot/local qualifier when verified:** Pilot terbatas Sukabumi

Avoid leading with “circular fashion.” It can exist in a deck or explanatory footer, but most users care first about the clothes, effort, and clarity of the process.

### Positioning test questions
Use these in five short user conversations before locking public wording:
1. When you hear “PindahTangan,” what service do you expect?
2. Does “konsinyasi fesyen terkelola” clarify it or sound too formal?
3. Which matters more: someone handles the selling work, or the clothes get a next owner?
4. What would you need to know before entrusting clothes to this service?
5. Does this feel like a local service you would ask about, or an online thrift shop?

**Pass signal:** participants can explain the service as “titip jual pakaian, mereka yang urus prosesnya” without being prompted.  
**If it fails:** retain the name but use the plain descriptor “titip jual pakaian yang dibantu dari awal sampai laku” in acquisition copy, then test again.

## 3. Visual identity direction

### Direction: **The Wardrobe Edit**
A warm, orderly fashion workspace. The visual world is an edit in progress: a garment on a rail, a hand checking fabric, a label, folded details, a seller preparing a bag. It should feel composed but lived-in.

This keeps the existing warm editorial foundation while avoiding two category traps:
- **Thrift-store cliché:** crowded racks, price-sticker chaos, grunge filters, random retro patterns.
- **Generic green sustainability:** leaves, recycle arrows, earthy slogans, all-beige “eco” stock imagery.

### Mark direction
Start with a strong typographic wordmark. A custom or carefully set “PindahTangan” wordmark should be the hero, with an optional small **PT** monogram only for favicons, hangtags, and compact app states.

Do not use a hanger, circular arrows, leaf, or hands-shaped logo as the primary mark. They explain too literally and age the brand quickly.

### Colour system
The current linen / espresso / terracotta foundation is right. Refine its role rather than add more decoration.

| Token | Hex | Role |
|---|---:|---|
| Linen | `#FAF7F2` | Primary canvas, breathing room |
| Paper | `#FFFFFF` | Cards, forms, documents |
| Espresso | `#1C1917` | Primary type, primary actions |
| Cocoa | `#44403C` | Body copy and secondary information |
| Clay | `#C25E43` | Brand accent, links, small status emphasis |
| Soft clay | `#FBE8E5` | Accent surfaces, selected states |
| Moss | `#849078` | Secondary utility accent only; never a “green claim” signal |
| Line | `#E7E2D8` | Borders and dividers |
| Caution | `#9A6700` | Human review / pending state, paired with text |

**Ratio:** 70% linen/paper, 20% espresso/cocoa, 8% clay, 2% moss or caution. Clay should guide the eye, not coat the screen.

### Type
- **Display:** Playfair Display or a similarly high-legibility editorial serif. Use sparingly for hero lines, section titles, editorial captions, and price moments.
- **UI/body:** Plus Jakarta Sans. Use sentence case and a calm hierarchy.
- **Data/operations:** a restrained monospace only for item IDs, dates, transaction references, and operational metadata.

Do not set long paragraphs in serif, do not use all-caps for normal UI, and do not make every headline italic. Editorial is a rhythm, not a font pairing.

### Interface principles
1. **Proof before flourish:** show status, condition, item details, and agreed amount plainly.
2. **One focal action per screen:** one dark primary CTA; clay supports, never competes.
3. **Use garments as evidence:** a product photo, condition note, and size beat abstract lifestyle art.
4. **Operations may be sharper:** admin/studio uses the same palette but tighter spacing, clearer labels, and less serif.
5. **Accessibility:** keep body copy espresso/cocoa on linen/paper; do not use clay text for paragraphs or encode statuses by colour alone.

### Photo and art direction
**Show:** Indonesian/Sukabumi-adjacent everyday style; real wardrobe variety; a single garment, fabric, button, label, rack, folding table, pickup bag, hands; natural window/studio light; honest texture and colour.

**Composition:** medium-close crops, one clear subject, practical context, air around the garment. Use a clean editorial crop rather than an aspirational luxury campaign pose.

**Do not show:** anonymous piles of used clothes, landfill/recycling imagery, exaggerated “before/after” clutter, fake courier scenes, imported-model stock that has no relationship to the service, or excessive dust/grain filters.

**If using AI imagery:** never depict a completed pickup, authenticated buyer, payout, hygienic treatment, or facility as if it were PindahTangan’s actual operation. Use it only as an explicitly conceptual mood reference until real photography exists.

## 4. Voice guide

### Voice profile
PindahTangan sounds like a capable friend who has already thought through the practical bits. It is friendly, clear, lightly fashionable, and not overly clever.

| Do | Avoid |
|---|---|
| “Pakaianmu masih bagus. Kalau kamu mau, kita bantu urus proses titip jualnya.” | “Saatnya upgrade lemari dan unlock cuanmu.” |
| “Setelah kami cek, kamu akan menerima informasi item yang diterima dan nilai bersih yang disepakati.” | “Kami memaksimalkan value setiap item dengan sistem revolusioner.” |
| “Mau tanya dulu sebelum ikut pilot? Boleh banget.” | “Join the movement sekarang juga.” |
| “Status masih menunggu konfirmasi.” | “Aman, pasti segera cair.” |

### Language rules
- Write in Indonesian first. Use familiar words: titip jual, pakaian, cek, proses, nilai bersih, status, jadwal.
- “Kamu” is the default for consumer surfaces; “Anda” only for formal receipts, terms, or sensitive finance communication.
- Use “kami” when PindahTangan takes responsibility. Do not overuse “kita” where ownership is unclear.
- Name uncertainty plainly: “sedang diuji,” “akan dikonfirmasi,” “setelah pembayaran pembeli terverifikasi.”
- Use “cuan” only as light campaign flavour, never in payout terms, receipts, or promised outcomes.
- Avoid em-dash constructions that try to sound profound; say the useful thing directly.

### Voice by moment
| Moment | Tone | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Invitation | Light, practical | “Ada pakaian yang masih bagus tapi nggak sempat kamu jual sendiri?” |
| Booking | Reassuring, exact | “Ceritakan perkiraan jumlah pakaian dan area kamu. Tim akan mengonfirmasi opsi penyerahan.” |
| QC / acceptance | Respectful, transparent | “Kami sudah selesai mengecek. Ini item yang bisa masuk proses titip jual dan nilai bersih yang diajukan.” |
| Live update | Lively, not chaotic | “Item ini masuk sesi live berikutnya. Detail jadwal akan kami kirim setelah dikonfirmasi.” |
| Payout | Formal, factual | “Pembayaran untuk item yang memenuhi syarat pencairan sedang direkonsiliasi.” |
| Problem / delay | Direct, accountable | “Ada kendala pada [hal]. Kami sedang cek dan akan memberi pembaruan paling lambat [waktu].” |

## 5. Messaging hierarchy

### Level 1: the memorable idea
**Pakaian bagusmu masih bisa lanjut jalan.**

### Level 2: what PindahTangan does
**Titip jual pakaian dengan proses yang dibantu dari awal: cek, kurasi, tampilkan, dan pantau progresnya.**

### Level 3: functional proof points (only if operationally true)
- Ada proses penerimaan dan pengecekan item.
- Penitip menerima keputusan penerimaan dan nilai bersih yang disepakati sebelum item masuk penjualan.
- Setiap item dapat dilacak melalui status yang jelas.
- PindahTangan mengurus presentasi dan penjualan; pemilik tidak perlu melisting satu per satu.

### Level 4: pilot reassurance
**Pilot ini dibuat kecil dulu supaya prosesnya benar-benar rapi.**  
Use only in invitation/landing surfaces, coupled with what is currently available and what will be confirmed later.

### Proof hierarchy
1. **Process proof:** actual item receipt, QC record, agreed net-payout record, verified status update.
2. **Product proof:** real garment condition, measurements, photos, and written acceptance criteria.
3. **Human proof:** consented, attributable pilot feedback after it exists.
4. **Scale proof:** only after measured and auditable results exist.

Never reverse this order by leading with testimonials, “trusted by,” item counts, or payment claims that have not happened.

## 6. Landing-page copy recommendation

This is a copy direction for the D3 implementation/materials task, not an approval to publish. The current prototype must remove unverified guarantees, earnings estimates, and fictional testimonials before any external use.

### Recommended hero
**Eyebrow:** Titip jual pakaian • pilot terbatas Sukabumi  
**Headline:** Pakaian yang masih bagus, nggak harus diam di lemari.  
**Body:** PindahTangan membantu mengurus proses titip jual pakaian yang masih layak pakai—mulai dari pengecekan sampai item siap ditawarkan. Kamu bisa fokus ke hal lain; kami bantu rapikan prosesnya.  
**Primary CTA:** Tanya soal pilot  
**Secondary CTA:** Lihat cara kerjanya

Do not use “Jemput Lemari” as the dominant CTA until pickup areas, capacity, operator, and terms are actually confirmed. Where a booking flow exists, the CTA can be “Ajukan minat pilot” rather than a promise of a scheduled pickup.

### Suggested flow
1. **Hero:** the wardrobe tension + low-pressure inquiry CTA.
2. **How it works:** 3 simple steps: Ceritakan pakaianmu → Kami cek dan sepakati detail → Ikuti statusnya saat proses berjalan. Avoid claiming an exact cadence until proven.
3. **What makes it easier:** no individual listing, no back-and-forth negotiation per item, clear acceptance/payout explanation.
4. **What we accept:** a short, real, reviewable guide with condition and category boundaries. Link to fuller terms.
5. **Pilot note:** capacity, service area, and next-step confirmation; do not invent a studio address or pickup schedule.
6. **FAQ:** questions prospective consignors actually ask: what happens after submission, how item acceptance works, how net payout is agreed, what happens if an item is not accepted, when updates occur.
7. **CTA:** “Tanya soal pilot” / WhatsApp only after a real monitored contact path exists.

### Copy to retire from the current prototype
| Current territory | Why it must go | Safer interim direction |
|---|---|---|
| “Uang tunai ditransfer otomatis setiap Jumat sore” | Payout flow is simulated and unresolved until payment/bank reconciliation is proven | “Jadwal dan mekanisme pencairan akan dijelaskan sebelum item masuk proses.” |
| `>100°C`, “steril,” “higienitas terjamin,” “wangi butik” | Unsupported hygiene/performance claims | “Item akan melalui pengecekan dan penanganan sesuai proses pilot yang disepakati.” |
| Slider estimating Rp32.500–Rp52.500 per piece | An unsupported earnings claim based on hard-coded values | “Nilai bersih dibahas per item yang diterima, sebelum masuk penjualan.” |
| “Ratusan perempuan…” and named “kisah nyata” | Fictional social proof in current source | Remove the section; later use attributed, consented feedback only. |
| Exact pickup coverage, address, fixed 30-day/Friday/ceban terms | Repository values are not evidence of live operating capacity or approved terms | Show only after owner approval and operational verification. |

## 7. Messaging guardrails and claim register

| Claim type | Approved now? | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| “Konsinyasi fesyen terkelola” | Yes, as a proposed service description | Pair with actual pilot scope; do not imply live operation if still testing. |
| “Kami bantu proses titip jual” | Yes | Make the actual supported steps explicit. |
| “Nilai bersih disepakati sebelum item masuk penjualan” | Proposed pilot wording | Use only once the intake agreement and record truly exist. |
| “Penjemputan gratis” | No | Requires verified coverage, capacity, threshold, and terms. |
| Scheduled/automatic payout | No | Requires payment, refund/hold, bank, and reconciliation controls. |
| Sterilisation / hygiene assurance | No | Requires validated operational process, evidence, and reviewed wording. |
| Earnings range, sold-item count, on-time rate | No | Requires measured, attributable pilot data. |
| Testimonials / community trust | No | Requires consented, attributable feedback; never seed it. |
| “Preloved” / “second hand” | Context-dependent | Use in buyer discovery/search contexts only; not as the brand’s primary self-description. |

## 8. Creative brief for any next asset

**Objective:** make the first prospective consignor understand that PindahTangan takes on the fragmented work of resale, then give her one low-pressure next step.  
**Audience:** a busy Sukabumi wardrobe owner with usable clothes and no desire to create listings herself.  
**Single-minded message:** good clothes can move on without her having to become a marketplace seller.  
**Mandatories:** clear pilot qualifier where relevant; no invented proof; no hygiene, income, pickup, or payout promise unless the claim register permits it; one CTA.  
**Creative guardrails:** warm editorial workspace, garment-first photography, clean hierarchy, human Indonesian, no thrift/eco cliché.  
**Success criterion:** a viewer can correctly describe the service and name the next step without being told.

## 9. Founder taste decision

**Decision to make:** choose the visual temperature before identity production starts.

- **Recommended: Direction A — Wardrobe Edit.** Linen, espresso, restrained clay; light, composed, and operationally credible. It best supports trust, product UI, and local pilot testing.
- **Alternative: Direction B — More expressive market energy.** Add bolder colour and stronger live-commerce graphics. This may make buyer-facing content more energetic, but risks turning the consignor service into generic thrift/live-sale visual noise.

**Recommendation:** lock Direction A for the master brand. If pilot data shows buyer content needs more energy, create a separate live-session content layer rather than changing the core identity.

## 10. Implementation checklist

Before applying this guide to any public-facing asset:
- [ ] The service scope, operator, contact route, and terms have been verified.
- [ ] Every claim is checked against Section 7.
- [ ] Any price/payout language uses “agreed net payout,” not a guaranteed estimate or hidden deduction.
- [ ] No testimonial, count, location, schedule, or partnership is presented without source evidence.
- [ ] Hero has one primary CTA and explains the service in the first viewport.
- [ ] At mobile size, body text, CTAs, statuses, and form labels remain readable and distinct.
- [ ] The final asset has an owner for replies and exception handling.
